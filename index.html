Home water: cold and current

Cold: the Sound is about 54°F now and 45 to 48°F from November to March. Wear a 5/4 hooded suit with booties and gloves (Urban Surf's year-round Washington pick) or a drysuit, and assume every swim lasts longer than you expect.

Currents: in the main basin the flood runs south and the ebb runs north. On a north-wind day the flood carries you downwind toward Shilshole and West Point; the ebb runs against the wind, giving steeper chop but making it easier to hold ground. So in a northerly, prefer the ebb; in a southerly, the flood. Currents are strongest off points like West Point. Check NOAA current predictions before you go.

Golden Gardens: stay upwind; Shilshole Marina is directly downwind in a north wind. Carkeek: the wind often sits about 100 yards out and is flukey near the beach, so expect to drag or swim out, and to swim in if it dies. High tide leaves little beach.

West Point: great sensor, poor launch. The point has the strongest current nearby and the beach is a long walk from parking.

Self-rescue: sail with your buddy, carry a phone in a waterproof pouch, tell someone your plan, and practice Marek's controlled drifting (sail in the water to windward, hold the uphaul, nose across the wind) before you need it. Call 911 for Seattle Harbor Patrol or the Coast Guard.

E-bike trailers: the 125 L Starboard and the 145 L Dialer fit a two-wheel SUP or surfboard bike trailer; the 147 L and both longboards are long for city riding. Use two-piece masts (each section is about 2.3 to 2.5 m) and roll the sails. Carkeek's beach is over a footbridge across the railroad tracks, so check how the trailer handles the bridge before a windy day. Golden Gardens has parking and grass next to the beach.

      How the stars are calculated

      Stars come from the forecast wind numbers only, with the same rules for every spot and day.

      1. Input. The sustained wind range forecast for that spot and day, in mph. NWS and Environment Canada forecasts are in knots and were converted (1 kt is 1.15 mph). Temira's Gorge forecast is already in mph. Gusts are shown but not counted.
      2. Speed band. Take the midpoint of the range. Under 8 mph is 0 stars, 8 to 11 is 1, 11 to 14 is 2, 14 to 18 is 3, 18 to 23 is 4, 23 to 28 is 5. The same bands drive the "what to bring" kit table: foil or longboard below 14, the 125 L board from 18 up. Sustained 28 mph or more is overpowered and is capped at 4.
      3. Timing penalty. A window of about 2 hours or less (after 5 pm, or evening only) subtracts 3 stars, because rigging and launching use up most of it. A window of 3 to 4 hours (morning only, or dawn to mid-morning) subtracts 1. Afternoon, all-day or split windows subtract nothing.
      4. Confidence penalty. Subtract 1 star when the source itself states low confidence. This week that is NWS Pendleton's roughly 30% confidence in Monday's Cascade gap-wind speeds (Vantage, Desert Aire, Lake Wenatchee, Rimrock, Lake Cle Elum).
      5. Direction cap. The forecast direction must be within 45° of a good direction listed for that spot (from nw-windtalk and the spot guides). If it is 45° or more away, the spot is capped at 1 star. Spots with no documented direction skip this step.
      6. Floor and gaps. Stars never go below 0. Days beyond a forecast's range (British Columbia Wednesday and Thursday) show a dash, not a guess.
      7. Distance bands. Spots are grouped by drive time from north Seattle: under 30 minutes, 30 minutes to 2½ hours, 2½ to 4 hours, and over 4 hours. A band's stars for a day equal its best spot that day. Ties go to the spot with the right wind direction, then to a sourced rather than estimated forecast, then to the shorter drive. "Browse by area" groups the same ratings by region instead.

      Discipline fit and gear. A spot-day "fits" a discipline from the same midpoint wind, as long as the direction is not capped and the window is not under 2 hours: longboard from 6 to 14 mph, foil from 8 to 18 mph, planing from 14 mph up. These come from windtalk reports (City League longboards racing in 3 to 18 mph, first foil flights in 8 to 10 kt, smooth foil flights at 13 to 17 mph, fin boards planing from the mid teens) and your gear's limits. Gear picks come from the "Your quiver" table at your weight.

      Not included: water temperature, tides, currents, swell, launch quality, crowds and the drive itself.

      Where the numbers come from: each spot's wind range comes from the forecast named in its "Basis" line: NWS marine forecasts and forecast discussions, Environment Canada marine forecasts, or Temira's Gorge forecast. Turning a zone forecast into one spot's local wind took judgment, and speeds marked "estimate" (Roads End) are my own inference from coast-wide forecasts. All the inputs, the formula and the results are saved with source ids in the database that goes with this page.
