Winduro

A long-distance race once around Mercer Island on Lake Washington, counterclockwise from Newcastle Beach Park, run by the nw-windtalk group on a south-wind day each fall. Longboards, windfoils and wingfoils race the same course, about 25 km by the shortest line.

What the tracks show

  • Stefan sailed the plan (1994 F2 Lightning raceboard, 8.5 m): rounded Mercer's north tip tight, reached across to the west opening in 21 min (the plan allows 20–24), and covered the beat from the Seward Slot to the south tip in 8.1 km and 39 min, finishing in 3:05, between the plan's target and steady times.
  • The north end cost the most time. After the north tip, John McC carried on toward Madrona and beat back to the west opening: 60 min for that leg, against 21 for Stefan and 42 for Montana.
  • Upwind, pointing beat speed. Stefan sailed 66° to the wind at 7.5 mph and made 2.9 mph straight upwind. John McC was nearly as fast (7.2 mph) but sailed lower (74°) and made 1.8 mph upwind; Montana pointed higher (69°) but slower (5.1 mph) and also made 1.8 mph. A few degrees higher at the same speed is the difference.
  • Timing mattered too. Stefan was on the big beat from 10:59 to 11:37, in the best wind of the day. John McC and Montana reached it after noon, as the wind faded and backed about 35°; John McC spent half an hour on the headed tack near the Slot.
  • Stefan's run home includes a repair. Stefan's 56 min from the south tip include 24 min getting the rig back on the board after a nose-dive (Stefan's report, below; the GPS shows Stefan nearly stopped for about 20 of them). Sailing took about 32 min; John McC took 47 and Montana 96. Without the repair Stefan's time would have been about 2:41, inside the plan's 2:47 target.

    Stefan Kinnestrand · 3:05

    Stefan's fourth Winduro, from Stefan's report on nw-windtalk (full post). Purple line on the map.

    Board and sailF2 Lightning, 8.5 m1994 F2 Lightning World Cup raceboard, Neil Pryde V8. Sailor 170 lb.
    TimeJust over 3 hoursIncluding 24 minutes getting the rig back on the board.
    DistanceAbout 21 milesGPS: Garmin Fenix 8 and GaiaGPS on an iPhone.
    BridgeWest openingStefan's first time on that side; in past years Stefan went under on the Mercer side.
    • Start to the north end. Started a few minutes after the main group and planed downwind all the way to the East Channel bridge. Lulls under and after the bridge, then wind again; by Mercer's north end Stefan was planing upwind on port tack, with strong wind coming out of the island's shadow.
    • The west opening. Stefan's port tack pointed straight at the western span, so Stefan went for it instead of tacking toward the Mercer side. Tacking under the bridge was no problem.
    • The big pond. Upwind across the "psycho chop" with a well-balanced rig and harness lines; a bit overpowered in the gusts but manageable. Near Mercer's south end Stefan caught Dean P (foil) and pulled ahead.
    • The incident. Past the south tip, on a deep beam reach and planing fast, the board went over a bigger swell and nose-dived. Stefan fell for the first time in the race and found the universal joint had come out of the mast track although the lock was still closed. The board blew downwind; Stefan swam it down and paddled back to the rig. Reattaching in deep water and chop took about 20 attempts: in the end Stefan locked the UJ into the track first, then knelt on the board and pushed the mast extension onto it. Twenty-four minutes from the fall to sailing again, about 10 minutes from the finish.
    • Stefan's take. After talking with Greg, Stefan thinks the UJ pin is an aftermarket part about 0.5 mm undersize, and that play in the locking pins let it lever out in the fall. Stefan will inspect them and look for a pin with a bigger base. In hindsight Stefan would have moved the mast back in the track before the downwind leg to keep the nose up, but the mast pedal is finicky, so that would have meant dropping the sail and uphauling again.

    For everyone

    • Check the UJ pin's fit and the locking mechanism before race day, especially on older boards and with non-original parts.
    • Move the mast track back before a broad reach in swell; a nose-dive is the most likely way to fall on the run home.
    • Know how to put a rig back on in deep water: lock the UJ into the track first, then fit the mast extension from your knees.

    Montana Harkin · 6:10

    A first Winduro, from Montana's report on nw-windtalk (full post; GPX and Strava in the follow-up). Orange line on the map. Kit: 5/4 wetsuit and an Ion vest carrying 2 L of water, snacks and a phone.

    Board and sailSuperlight, 8.0 mMistral Superlight, Sailworks Retro 8.0. Sailor 6'2", 215 lb.
    Time6 h 14 minBy Montana's watch, 9:26 (starting with John) to 3:40; 6:10 between the start and finish lines on the track.
    Distance24.6 milesTop speed 12.5 knots, on the broad reach right after the start.
    BridgeWest openingWalked under it with John.
    • The first downwind. Planed off the start, then found the downwind hard on the Superlight, with several uphauls. Greg sailed by and pointed out that the daggerboard needs to stay down on the downwind legs for stability; settling into an upright stance helped.
    • The north end. A broad reach Montana could sail well; the calm part of the day.
    • The west opening. Seeing the west end of the bridge, Montana pointed straight for it and caught John under it. They walked under together and had a snack, then tacked out (Greg suggests six tacks before crossing; they did four). A gust backwinded the sail and threw Montana down; after a couple more tacks came the longest reach of the day: fast, balanced, and where Montana learned the most about harness line placement.
    • The beat. Tacking up the island, Montana passed Maddie and then John, learning to sit into the harness to put more weight on the mast foot and stand further back.
    • Home. The downwind leg brought many falls; on the fourth or fifth, the mast base popped out and the board took off downwind. Chasing it, remounting the base and reattaching the sail took a few minutes, but the swell had gone by then.

    Lessons

    • Downwind on a Superlight: keep the daggerboard down for stability.
    • Sitting into the harness on the beats puts weight on the mast foot and lets you stand further back.
    • Montana's mast base came off in a fall as Stefan's did: check that yours locks firmly.

    Course guide

    Lessons for longboards, a line to sail with target times, leg-by-leg notes, past results and bail-outs.

    Lessons for longboards

    • Round Mercer's north tip tight. Then reach across to the west opening. Every boat length sailed north of the bridge has to be beaten back.
    • Point 60 to 65° to the wind on the beats. A longboard's speed barely changes between 60° and 90°, so the higher angle gets upwind about twice as fast. Below about 55° the sail stalls. Squeeze up a few degrees at a time, don't sheet in while heading up, and if it stalls, bear off about 10° to rebuild speed. In a 190° wind that's roughly 125 to 130° on starboard and 250 to 255° on port.
    • Sail the wind, not fixed headings. The wind can swing 30° or more during the beat. When a tack stops gaining ground upwind, tack.
    • Rail the board in the lulls. Under about 10 mph: daggerboard fully down, mast track forward, back foot across the centerline or in the front beating strap, toes down, board tilted onto its leeward rail (Marek and Greg on nw-windtalk).
    • Run home with the daggerboard up and the mast track back, gybing between broad reaches rather than sailing dead downwind.
    • Check the gear before race day: the daggerboard has no side-to-side play in the well and stays fully down under load, and the UJ pin fits snugly in the mast track and its lock holds (see Stefan's report). Arrive rested; it's a long day.

    Leg by leg

    • Start to the hi-rise (5–8 min, downwind). Fast. Mats (2022) passed a bit away from Mercer to gain on the boat hugging it.
    • Mercer's lee: up the East Channel and round the north tip (15–25 min). The big shadow. Watch for puffs off the Bellevue side, then the Mercer side; stay off the Luther Burbank shore. Don't burn energy pumping early.
    • North tip to the floating bridge (upwind). Round the tip tight to Mercer: in 2026 a longboard that carried on toward Madrona lost about 40 minutes against the planned line beating back. The wind comes back hard and gusty. Daggerboard down, rig forward, harness lines right. Make one long tack to the west opening rather than short-tacking the Mercer shore (Giovanni beat Mats that way in 2022).
    • Start line. If the buoy is gone, the line runs due west from the corner of the dock (Nikolay).
    • Through the west opening. Short tacks, well clear of the piers: behind a pillar the wind vanishes. If it gets ugly, you can walk your gear along the shore under the bridge here (Nikolay). Get away from the chop and reflected waves on the windward (south) side quickly.
    • The beat to the south tip (64–86 min for 2025 longboards). Daggerboard fully down and point. Right after the bridge, stay closer to the shore through the "psychochop"; it's flatter. In the big pond you can tack close to Mercer, but keep off Mercer's south end, a wind hole (Nikolay). Use the full width of the Slot between Seward Park and Mercer. Lay the south tip with one long tack instead of pinching.
    • Home (15–30 min). Run downwind to the dock; look for the long stretch of green trees and the light-green pavilion roof. If tired, don't risk gybes: Greg lost 1.5 hours to crashes and rests in 2025.
    • Kit: phone in a waterproof bag clipped twice, water and snacks, warm suit for 45–55°F and rain. Sign out at the library box when you finish.
